---
title: Equipment inference
---

Once a building's points are classified, much of its equipment model
follows. `src/baler/equip_inference.py` works that out, and **everything
it finds is a proposal with its evidence**, for a person to confirm in
[Mapping review](/mapping-review). Nothing is asserted silently.

## Equipment type from its point set

An equip with chilled water supply and return temperatures, a run status
and a start/stop command is a chiller.
- The signatures are Cx's own expected-point templates
  (`ontology/cx-expected-points.ttl`): AHU, VAV, FCU, chiller, boiler and
  pump. Two more cover kinds Cx doesn't template: electrical meters and
  fans.
- Each signature gets a score from two things: how many of its expected
  points the equip has (a required point counts double), and how much of
  the equip's point set the signature explains.
- For an equipment proposal, the [model step](/model-step) lists the top
  three as candidates, marked *points*, with a reason such as "has 4 of 4
  expected points (chilled water supply temperature, …)".

**Cx's expected-point check.** Suppose the ladder would auto-accept an
equip (by memory, the model or a site rule) as a class whose required
expected points are missing, such as an AHU with no fan status. That's
suspicious, so it is **vetoed** and stays a proposal. The check runs only
once every one of the equip's points is classified, because an
unclassified point may be the missing one. In the measurement below, a
looser threshold (80% classified) vetoed 6 correct answers on the mock
hospital.

## Relationships

`python -m baler.autotag` ends by proposing relationships, which appear in
the review page's **Equipment relationships** section:

- **Grouping (`hasPoint`).** Points with no equipment are grouped by their
  topic's parent (a device or prefix group), and each group is proposed as
  one piece of equipment. Accepting creates the equipment and links its
  points.
- **`feeds`.** A VAV's discharge air temperature that tracks one AHU's
  supply air temperature suggests that AHU feeds it.
  - The test is Pearson's r over 5-minute means of the last 3 days.
  - It proposes only when r ≥ 0.8 over at least 24 shared samples, and the
    best AHU beats the next by at least 0.1.
  - Accepting writes `brick:feeds` and `brick:isFedBy`.

Accepted relationships are logged in `record_edits`. The API is
`GET /ops/mapping/relationships` (viewer) and
`POST /ops/mapping/relationships/decide` (operator).

## Measured

On the [mapping eval](/mapping-eval)'s mock hospital (88 pieces of
equipment):

| | Equipment: true class is the top candidate | Equipment exact, auto-accepted |
|---|---|---|
| Rules only, before | 77.3% | 0% |
| Rules only, with point-set inference | **84.1%** | 0% (still proposals) |
| With memory from seed 7 | n/a | 100%, 0 wrong (the Cx check vetoes nothing correct) |

With the **true** point classes, inference alone gets the exact class for
74 of 88 pieces of equipment and is never wrong. The other 14 come out
less specific. Six pumps come out as `Pump` rather than `Chilled_Water_Pump`
or `Hot_Water_Pump`, because their points are identical. Eight exhaust fans
come out as `Fan` rather than `Exhaust_Fan`, because a fan status and
command don't say exhaust. The label or a person settles those.

**Not measured:** `feeds` and grouping on real data. The mock hospital
has no VAV discharge temperatures, and its history is one reading per
point. Every point there is already on a piece of equipment. Unit tests
cover both with synthetic series and graphs: the right AHU is picked, a tie
proposes nothing, and too few samples propose nothing. Their first real
test is a site with history.
