> Ten years after the building is built, a building engineer can pin-point
> a hunting motor.

Hunting is a control loop oscillating: sustained or on and off. It costs
energy, actuator wear and setpoint tracking. NIST's BAS-data FDD method
flags "unstable, excessively oscillatory regulation"; Chintala et al.
(ASHRAE 2015) found it common in AHU trend data from ten buildings; Price
et al. (*Buildings*, 2022) show field fixes. Baler measures it per loop,
against the equipment's own start-up baseline, not a global number
(Baler#214, epic #221 §8.1).

It runs over what Baler already has: raw `point_history`, the Cx ladder's
loop pairs, Cx 6 start-up tests and the fault rules. No new pipeline.

## The metrics

`baler/oscillation.py`, shared by the fault rule and the report:

| Metric | What it is |
|---|---|
| `reversals_per_hour` | direction changes of a command or position, counting only moves larger than the deadband |
| `crossings_per_hour` | sign changes of the error (sensor − setpoint), with hysteresis: it must pass beyond ± the deadband |
| `period_s` | twice the median time between reversals |
| `amplitude` | median half peak-to-peak between reversals |
| `travel_per_hour` | total movement per hour: actuator wear |
| `median_interval_s` | the sampling it was judged on |

**Sampling matters.** A 4-minute oscillation is invisible in 15-minute
averages. A series sampled slower than once a minute is reported
`judgeable: false` with the reason, and nothing is concluded. That is why
Baler keeps raw history compressed instead of downsampling it
([History compression](/history-compression)), and why loop points want a
COV or fast sampling profile at the edge (Windrow#27). A COV point that
genuinely doesn't move also reports sparse samples, and is shown as not
judgeable rather than as steady.

## Per loop, against the start-up baseline

```bash
curl "$BALER/cx/projects/$P/hunting?hours=24&days=7"
```

- **Loops** are the setpoint pairs the Cx ladder finds (supply air
  temperature, zone temperature, static pressure, airflow), each with the
  equipment's analog commands and position feedbacks (valve, damper, speed,
  frequency, position). Equipment with commands but no setpoint pair still
  gets its commands measured.
- **Now**: the metrics over the last `hours`.
- **Baseline**: the same over the window of the equipment's first completed
  start-up or functional test (Cx 6). Until the asset registry lands (#197)
  that is the position's first test; then it becomes the installed asset's,
  so a replaced motor is never compared against the old one.
- **Verdict**: `hunting` when the loop's rate (its worst command's
  reversals/h, else the error's crossings/h) is over `ratio` (default 3) ×
  the baseline's, or over `min_reversals_per_hour` (default 12) with no
  baseline; `steady`; or `inconclusive` when nothing could be judged.
- **Onset**: the first day of the current run of days above that threshold,
  looking back up to `days`.
- **What changed**: setpoint edits in the setpoint's own history within a day
  of the onset, and install/remove events from the asset registry
  (`hunting.ASSET_EVENTS`, empty until #197).

Deadbands are settings, not truths: `command_deadband` (default 2, in % for
0–100 commands) and `error_deadband` (default 0.5, in the variable's units).
The thresholds are configurable for the same reason. They are starting
points, not values from a standard.

## As a fault rule

A His-mode `hunting` rule in `rules.json` / `POST /fault/rules` runs the same
metric on every point it applies to, opens a fault (with the metrics in its
detail) and fires the fault webhooks:

```json
{"name": "chw-valve-hunting", "mode": "his", "type": "hunting",
 "applies_to": {"brick_class": "Valve_Command"},
 "window_seconds": 3600, "max_reversals_per_hour": 12, "deadband": 2.0}
```

See [Fault detection](/fault-detection).

## Measured, not claimed

The tests use synthetic series (a 4-minute, ±22 % sine at 30 s). This page
quotes no field detection rate: that needs real loop data with known
hunting, which we don't have yet.
