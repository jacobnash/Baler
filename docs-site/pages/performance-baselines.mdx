title: Performance baselines

> Show what the chiller has lost in efficiency since it was installed.

A performance baseline is a model of how a piece of equipment performed
when it was known good, so that later performance can be compared with
*itself*, not with a brochure (Baler#215, epic #221 §8.2). Chiller kW/ton
comes first.

## The chiller model

Fitted on hourly averages over a period you choose, normally the first
cooling season after acceptance:

```
kW/ton = b0 + b1·tons + b2·tons² + b3·ECWT
```

Load and entering condenser water temperature (ECWT) are in the model so
that a hot week, which raises ECWT and kW/ton, isn't mistaken for a worn
chiller.

- **Inputs** are existing points, read as hourly averages through the
  history rollups (`timeseries.read_rollup`, the continuous aggregates;
  raw history stays raw). `kw` (chiller power, kW) and `ecwt` (degF), and
  either `tons` (any point in tons, for example a [derivation](/derivation)'s
  output) or chilled-water `chw_flow_gpm`, `chw_supply_f` and `chw_return_f`
  (tons = gpm × ΔT / 24).
- Hours below 10 % of the period's peak load are left out: kW/ton near zero
  load is noise.
- At least 48 usable hours are needed, and a period that doesn't vary in
  load or ECWT is refused (there is nothing to fit).

## Model quality

Every baseline reports ASHRAE Guideline 14's calibration statistics,
**CV(RMSE)** and **NMBE**, with `meets_thresholds` against thresholds that
are **settings**: 30 % and ±10 % by default, labelled "verify against the
current edition of ASHRAE Guideline 14". Baler doesn't quote them as the
standard's numbers. Check them against the edition you work to.

## Degradation

```bash
curl -X POST $BALER/cx/projects/$P/baselines -d '{
  "equip_uri": "urn:equip:fbf/plant/ch-1",
  "points": {"kw": "urn:point:.../kw", "tons": "urn:point:.../tons", "ecwt": "urn:point:.../ecwt"},
  "fit_start": "2027-05-01T00:00:00Z", "fit_end": "2027-09-30T00:00:00Z"}'

curl "$BALER/cx/baselines/$B/degradation?bucket=month&alert_pct=10"
```

For each month (or week) after the fit, the report gives actual and
predicted kW/ton and the gap in %, **at matched conditions only**: hours
whose load and ECWT are outside the range the model was fitted on are not
compared (no extrapolation), and the report counts them
(`hours_outside_fit_range`). A positive gap is more energy per ton than
the baseline.

`alert_pct` is your threshold. Industry rules of thumb ("inspect at 10 %
drift") are vendor claims, so Baler has no default and presents none as a
standard.

## Baselines belong to the asset

A refit supersedes the active baseline for the same equipment and asset;
nothing is deleted. Each baseline has an `asset_id` for when the asset
registry lands (#197): a replaced chiller gets its own baseline instead of
being compared against the machine it replaced.

## Measured, not claimed

The tests use a synthetic chiller following the model form with 1 % noise,
an 8 % drift after the fit season, and a hot spell outside the fitted
range. The report finds a gap between 7 % and 9 % and leaves the hot spell
out. No accuracy on real plant data is claimed yet.
